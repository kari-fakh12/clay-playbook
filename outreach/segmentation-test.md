# The 16-segment outreach test

## Why I split it up

If you send one campaign to everyone and 3% reply, you've learned almost nothing. Was it the role? The message? The type of account?

So I split the leads into 16 segments before sending anything. Each lead sits in exactly one cell, and each cell has its own HeyReach campaign. When something works, I know which cell it came from.

## The grid

Four buyer roles times four tiers of how long the company has been on its HR system.

| Years on system | CHRO | CFO | IT / technical | All other ICP (global payroll, HRIS) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 1: 10+ years | segment 1 | segment 2 | segment 3 | segment 4 |
| Tier 2: 5 to 10 years | segment 5 | segment 6 | segment 7 | segment 8 |
| Tier 3: under 5 years | segment 9 | segment 10 | segment 11 | segment 12 |
| Tier 4: newest customers | segment 13 | segment 14 | segment 15 | segment 16 |

The tenure tier and role label are set in Clay (system 02), so the split is there before the leads ever reach HeyReach.

## What I measured

Per segment:
- connection requests sent
- acceptance rate
- replies
- interested leads

All of it went into a live program report for the client: an acceptance heat map by role and tier, the message bank, and the next-step plan.

## How the grid gets used

- **A column that doesn't reply gets cut.** If a role accepts connections but never talks, I stop spending on that column.
- **Budget follows acceptance.** The roles that accept most get the next batch of leads.
- **Tenure tiers get compared.** If one tier runs warmer, it gets priority when the lead pools are refreshed.
- **Messages get tested per cell.** Each cell can run a different angle, so the strongest message is picked from real replies, not from what sounds good.

The actual numbers belong to the client, so they're not in here.
